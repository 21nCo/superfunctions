# Stream renewal reply deadline fencing

The shared stream DeliveryLease scheduled a deadline timer but accepted renewal success against only the renewal request's start plus duration. A promise microtask can execute before an overdue timer after event-loop delay. In that ordering, a reply past the previously confirmed deadline could cancel the old timer and revive the lease. An overdue renewal timer could also issue a new renewal before the deadline timer ran.

Two controlled-clock unit regressions failed before correction: a 90ms lease begins renewal at 30ms, then receives success at monotonic 100ms before its overdue timer is dispatched; and the renewal callback itself first executes at monotonic 100ms. Six existing lease tests passed in the same baseline. These are deterministic scheduler-order reproductions, not claims about measured host scheduling latency.

The lease now records its confirmed deadline. It checks that deadline before issuing renewal and again before accepting a positive reply, in addition to the existing request-start-plus-duration bound. A reply past either bound loses ownership once, aborts the signal and removes owned timers. No lease duration or native test timeout changed. Existing transient-error retry, explicit stop, callback-return stop-renewal and no-revival behavior remain.
